# Maternal-Mortality-in-Africa
Exploring maternal mortality patterns and associated factors across African countries using Power BI and Excel.


## Project Overview

Maternal mortality remains a major public health concern, particularly in Sub-Saharan Africa, which accounted for approximately 70% of global maternal deaths in 2023 according to the World Health Organization.

This project explores maternal mortality across African countries and examines factors showing the strongest association with maternal mortality, with a focus on health-system and nutritional indicators.

The data was sourced from the **WHO Global Health Observatory** and included eight indicators.

## Objectives

* Explore maternal mortality patterns across African countries.
* Identify indicators showing the strongest association with maternal mortality.
* Examine geographical variation in maternal mortality.
* Present the findings through an interactive Power BI dashboard.

## Tools & Methods

**Tools**
- Microsoft Excel
- Power BI
- Power Query

**Data preparation**

* Filtered datasets to African countries.
* Selected relevant categories from datasets with subcategory breakdowns.
* Retained the most recent available observation for each country.
* Removed unnecessary columns and standardized column names.
* Connected the cleaned datasets using a central country lookup table in Power BI.

**Analysis**

* Correlation analysis was conducted in Excel to examine the relationship between maternal mortality and the selected indicators.
* Power BI was used to create the interactive dashboard and geographic visualizations.

## Key Findings

* **Skilled birth attendance** showed the strongest association with maternal mortality among the indicators analysed, with a correlation coefficient of approximately **-0.61**.
* Skilled birth attendance, institutional births, Caesarean-section prevalence and antenatal-care coverage showed negative correlations with maternal mortality.
* Anaemia among pregnant women, adolescent birth rates and anaemia among women of reproductive age showed positive correlations with maternal mortality.
* Maternal mortality showed substantial variation across African countries.
* Skilled birth attendance coverage also varied considerably between countries, with some countries recording particularly low coverage.

> **Note:** These findings represent correlations observed at the country level and should not be interpreted as evidence of causation.


## Limitations

* Indicator years varied across datasets, so the analysis does not represent a single-year snapshot of maternal mortality across Africa.
* The data should not be interpreted as representing the precise state of maternal mortality in 2026.
* The analysis uses country-level data, so the observed relationships may not reflect individual-level relationships.
* Correlation does not establish causation, and other factors not included in the analysis may also influence maternal mortality.
